THE OPPOSITION HAS SPOKEN

There is nothing more useful than a good disagreement — provided it happens after you've made up your own mind.

At Lingfield, the Racing Post camp has a strong case for No Return in the 3.50. We have gone the other way with Elvetham. At Wolverhampton the press room has support for Variety Act in the 6.20, while our card is headed by Booziebrunch.

Then there is Ripon's 3.40, where peace unexpectedly breaks out. Outside opinion is warm on Spirit Of Applause after a luckless latest run. He was already sitting at the top of our own three.

None of that changes the selections. That's the point. Tuesday gives us a proper test of where independent views agree — and where one side of the press room is going to have to buy the other a drink.

COURSE WATCH — SOFTLY, SOFTLY AT GOWRAN

Gowran's soft ground is the material conditions story of the day. It is not enough simply to take a recent figure achieved on quicker ground and pretend the surface beneath the horse has not changed.

The card has therefore been judged with a stronger emphasis on proven form under testing or near-testing conditions. Carvalhal and Faoladh are two horses whose cases became considerably more interesting once the rain entered the equation, while several apparently obvious quicker-ground profiles became less secure.

With big fields in several races, this still isn't a meeting for false certainty. It is a meeting for respecting the ground.

MONDAY'S CHEERFUL AUTOPSY — THE BOXES LIE

Well, that was ridiculous. Six horses were carefully lifted from Monday's card, polished, presented in the headline boxes and invited to demonstrate the quality of The Daily Winner's work.

Not one of them won. Main Tips 0/3. Value Tips 0/3. Boxes 0/6. Excellent work, everybody.

Underneath the smoking remains of the boxes, the British number ones won eight times from 30 eligible runners — including Prince Ali at 10/1 and Travellers Girl at 7/1 — and a theoretical £1 on every number one returned a £9.88 profit at SP.

The wider ranking was better still: the winner appeared somewhere in our Top 3 in 19 of 31 British races. Only Racing Post Ratings found more outright winners in the national press comparison, beating us 9–8.

The lesson was not to rebuild the engine. It was to be more selective about which races deserve the shop window. Small-field maidens, evidence-thin novices and tactically awkward races now have to work much harder to earn one of only three premium boxes.

Diagnosis: engine purring, bodywork needs attention. Treatment: fewer silly races in expensive boxes. Prognosis: we go again Tuesday.
